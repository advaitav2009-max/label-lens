Food Additive & INS Code Standards

A systematic reference for decoding the International Numbering System (INS), industrial preservatives, and hidden sugar derivatives.

1. International Numbering System (INS) Framework

The INS system (aligned with European E-numbers) assigns standardized numerical identifiers to food additives. When manufacturing declarations cite "INS 621" rather than "Monosodium Glutamate", or "INS 102" rather than "Tartrazine", consumers are frequently unaware of the chemical compounds being ingested.

100 – 199
Coloring Agents
Synthetic pigments and dyes (e.g. INS 102 Tartrazine, INS 129 Red 40, INS 150d Caramel IV).
200 – 299
Preservatives
Antimicrobial inhibitors (e.g. INS 211 Sodium Benzoate, INS 220 Sulfites, INS 250 Sodium Nitrite).
300 – 399
Antioxidants & Regulators
Lipid stabilization (e.g. INS 300 Ascorbic Acid, INS 319 TBHQ, INS 322 Lecithin).
600 – 699
Flavor Enhancers
Savory neuro-enhancers (e.g. INS 621 MSG, INS 627 Guanylate, INS 631 Inosinate).
900 – 999
Artificial Sweeteners
Intense sweeteners and glazes (e.g. INS 951 Aspartame, INS 950 Ace-K, INS 955 Sucralose).

2. Sugar Fragmentation & Synonyms

Regulatory standards mandate ingredients be ordered by decreasing weight. Manufacturers frequently split refined sugars across multiple chemical derivatives to suppress sugar from appearing in primary positions:

High Fructose Corn Syrup Invert Sugar Syrup Maltodextrin Dextrose Liquid Glucose Corn Syrup Solids Barley Malt Extract Fruit Juice Concentrate Agave Nectar Treacle Molasses Sucrose Maltose

3. Processed Lipids: Palm Olein & Hydrogenated Fats

Commercial formulations frequently utilize fractionated palm olein or interesterified vegetable fats due to thermal stability and minimal cost. These substances are concentrated in saturated palmitic acid and can carry trace industrial trans-fatty acid contaminants.

Clinical Observation: Prioritize products manufactured with expeller-pressed seed oils, pure butterfat, or cold-pressed olive oils over hydrogenated palm kernel oils.

4. LabelLens 5-Star Health Rating Structure

Ratings are computed algorithmically based on ingredient purity, NOVA processing levels, additive risk classifications, and lipid profiles:

4.5 – 5.0 Stars (Score 90 – 98) Strongly Recommended (100% Pure Formulation)

100% natural whole foods with zero synthetic additives, zero added sugars, zero industrial fats, and concise natural ingredient lists (e.g. green tea & dandelion root, 100% rolled oats, raw nuts, seeds, fresh milk).

4.0 – 4.4 Stars (Score 80 – 89) Strongly Recommended

Wholesome multi-ingredient formulations made with authentic culinary ingredients, free from artificial preservatives and industrial trans or palm lipids.

3.5 – 3.9 Stars (Score 70 – 79) Good Choice / Safe to Eat

Standard packaged foods with safe everyday culinary leaveners or acidity regulators (e.g. baking soda INS 500, citric acid INS 330) and modest natural sugar or fat.

2.5 – 3.4 Stars (Score 50 – 69) Consume in Moderation

Contains refined industrial seed oils, palm lipids/palmolein, or multiple sugar synonyms. Any formulation containing palm oil or trans fats is strictly capped at maximum 2.9 stars.

1.0 – 2.4 Stars (Score 10 – 49) Not Recommended / Ultra-Processed

Heavy chemical additive load, flagged synthetic dyes (e.g. Tartrazine), hydrogenated trans fats, or high industrial processing markers. Non-food skincare items receive 1.0 star (0 score) as inedible.
